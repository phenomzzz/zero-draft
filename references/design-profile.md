# Design profile

This skill's default preferences. They are quality bars a direction must meet, not a visual style and not universal UX rules. Explicit instructions, audience needs, accessibility and an established identity take precedence. Do not update personal memory or this profile implicitly.

| Preference | Apply it by | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distinctive work for this business | Identity traced to the subject's material, audience and content; hierarchy through scale, contrast, imagery and composition. Boldness is a degree, reachable in any genre or value | Generic card grids, uniformly tiny headings, luxury clichés, the house signature below by default |
| Strong fundamentals | Shared edges, deliberate grouping, coherent spacing/type scale and actual wrap checks | Random whitespace, misaligned rails, cramped CTAs, accidental widows and clipping |
| Motion with a role | Physics chosen for the concept, spatial logic, pacing that never delays a task, immediate control feedback | One fade-up applied everywhere, flashes, excessive bounce, restarting from zero |
| Seamless exploration | Keep context while inspecting alternatives; direct paths and reversible choices | Forced page hops, hidden information, duplicated navigation and scroll traps |
| A consistent product | The same language for calendar, forms, nav, footer, drawers and empty states | A polished hero followed by default widgets and neglected secondary pages |
| Concrete reference research | Observe real sites/interactions and explain what transfers | Calling a pattern tested or state of the art because a gallery endorses it |
| Content-first proportion | Scannable practical details and proportionate disclosure | Preview disclaimers in the composition, ornamental sections and repeated copy |
| Convincing photography | Inspect originals, crop, subject and resolution in place | Upscaled thumbnails, irrelevant photos, empty tracks and stretched imagery |
| Candid, autonomous collaboration | Recommend, implement authorized work, show what is ready and verified | Flattery, vague assurances, repeated permission prompts and stopping at a plan |

## House signature: check before committing

Four isolated builds of one brief (a game-community site, 15 October 2026) and the placeholder styling of a local animation-demo library share one grammar. Treat it as this skill's likely default for any brief until trials show otherwise:

1. Near-black ground with one saturated accent.
2. Condensed sans display face over a neutral sans.
3. Two- or three-line declarative headlines ending in a period, one word or line in the accent.
4. Tiny tracked or monospace uppercase micro-labels.
5. Elements rising 20–50px with an ease-out fade as the main motion.
6. An oversized wordmark as hero, repeated as the footer.

The 2026-10-16 trial measured the inverse default: with this list active, 3 of 4 builds chose a light greige ground, Archivo at 125% width, underlined text-link nav, a square near-black button and no accent, and a blind grader sorted them as one author across three briefs. Avoiding these six traits is not divergence.

Before committing a direction, list the traits it uses and the subject, asset or brief reason for each. "Official renders with emissive effects need a dark stage" is a reason; "fits the genre", "modern" or "premium" is not. Derive the shell, not only the centerpiece. For ground, display face, navigation and buttons, write the subject reason. If the page with its centerpiece removed would sort with another brief's page, re-derive the shell from the subject ([research-and-direction.md](research-and-direction.md)). When earlier builds exist in the workspace, compare tokens and a thumbnail: if the new direction would sort into the same pile without a subject reason, revise. Traits are justified, not banned; an unjustified opposite is equally weak.

Do not use this list to choose between tiles. Reject a tile for a missing subject reason, never for resemblance alone; a dark tile for a dark material (volcanic stone, a planetarium's night programme) may win.

A past project's own choices (its palette, type pairing, page structure, signature transitions and scroll behavior) belong to that project. They are not defaults either.

## Collaboration

- A reference offered for its UX quality (alignment, spacing, flow) is not a request to copy its aesthetic; state which attributes transfer.
- Give one justified recommendation when evidence supports it; show consequential alternatives where useful.

## Turn taste into acceptance criteria

Replace "make this premium" with observable targets. Example: the selected bike, its size and its price are visible together; chosen and browsed items differ clearly; the heading stays stable while content enters; image coverage never exposes a blank strip; the drawer preserves page position and restores focus.

Separate observed defects (a button is unreachable on a short screen), design recommendations (a pinned chosen card helps this comparison) and preferences (a quieter reveal suits the tone). Do not disguise the latter two as proven research.

Keep approved and rejected examples with reasons in the project's decision log: screenshots for composition, recordings for motion. Keep client assets and personal credentials out of portable examples.
